Finding medicines that don't exist yet.

For diseases with no current treatment, Moleculon runs computer simulations of how candidate biological reagents might behave against a disease before any lab or patient testing begins — exploring far more compounds than time and lab budgets would normally allow.

  • In-silico screening of thousands of candidate compounds
  • Narrowing to the handful most likely to become a working medicine
  • Moving promising candidates toward trials faster
  • Built to sit on top of existing lab-research infrastructure, not replace it

Why now

Three things changed at once.

01

The models got good enough

TrialGPT already shows 87% criterion-level accuracy on trial matching and cuts manual screening time by 40%+. Novartis has generated 10,000+ clinical documents with AI across 150+ trials.

02

The regulator gave a framework

FDA CDER published draft AI guidance in Jan 2025, stood up an AI Council in 2024, and issued 10 joint FDA–EMA AI principles in Jan 2026 — a risk-based framework that promotes innovation, not a moratorium.

03

The vendor landscape stopped short

Recruitment tools like Deep 6 AI and Antidote stop at matching. Safety-database incumbents bolt AI onto decades-old systems. No one owns the connective tissue across all four operational functions.

Execution plan

Two pilots, real data, one year.

Phase 1 — Narrow & prove

Build and pilot the safety-report agent with one hospital or research partner using real, anonymized trial data. Prove it cuts review time and produces a reliable, human-checked record.

Phase 1 (parallel) — Research proof point

Begin the simulation-research tool with a university lab studying a disease with no current treatment, so both halves of Moleculon have real-world proof points early.

Phase 2 — Expand on evidence

Use before-and-after numbers from both pilots to expand to more trials, hospitals, and disease-research partners citywide.

Throughout — Partner, don't replace

Integrate with existing safety-database and lab-research providers rather than replacing systems outright. Keep a human expert in the loop at every step.

Year one goal

One validated pilot on each side of Moleculon, ready to expand citywide.

Competition & risk

The honest view.

Veeva, Oracle Argus, IQVIA and ArisGlobal have the client relationships and compliance credibility to bolt on agentic features before a new entrant builds a comparable data moat. The counter: legacy schemas and workflows built around individual steps make incumbents structurally slow to rebuild around agent-native architecture.

FDA's AI framework is still a draft and could tighten. This argues for building an unusually strong audit-trail and explainability layer from the start — a cost early, a moat later.

Pharma is a conservative buyer when errors carry patient-safety consequences. This is the strongest argument for human-in-the-loop design and for launching with a partner willing to co-develop and publicly validate the product.
